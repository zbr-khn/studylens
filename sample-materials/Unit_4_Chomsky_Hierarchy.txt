## Section 4.1: Introduction to Formal Grammars
A formal grammar G is mathematically defined as a 4-tuple:
G = (V, \Sigma, R, S)
where:
- V is a finite set of non-terminal characters (variables).
- \Sigma is a finite set of terminal symbols disjoint from V (alphabet).
- R is a finite relation of production rules mapping strings in (V \cup \Sigma)* to strings in (V \cup \Sigma)*.
- S \in V is the designated start variable.

## Section 4.2: The Chomsky Hierarchy Overview
Noam Chomsky classified formal grammars into four distinct nested levels based on the structural constraints imposed on their production rules:

### Type-0: Recursively Enumerable / Unrestricted Grammars
- Production Rule Format: \alpha \to \beta, where \alpha \in (V \cup \Sigma)+ and \beta \in (V \cup \Sigma)*.
- Recognizing Automaton: Turing Machine (TM).
- Properties: Languages recognized by Turing machines; undecidable in general (Halting problem).

### Type-1: Context-Sensitive Grammars (CSG)
- Production Rule Format: \alpha A \beta \to \alpha \gamma \beta, where A \in V, \alpha, \beta, \gamma \in (V \cup \Sigma)*, and |\gamma| \ge 1.
- Crucial Constraint: The replacement of variable A with string \gamma occurs strictly in the presence of left context \alpha and right context \beta. Furthermore, rules are non-contracting: |\alpha| \le |\beta| for all rules \alpha \to \beta (except optionally S \to \epsilon if S does not appear on right-hand side).
- Recognizing Automaton: Linear Bounded Automaton (LBA) - a non-deterministic Turing machine whose tape is bounded by a constant multiple of the input length.
- Canonical Example: The non-context-free language L = {a^n b^n c^n | n \ge 1} is generated by a Type-1 context-sensitive grammar.

### Type-2: Context-Free Grammars (CFG)
- Production Rule Format: A \to \gamma, where A \in V is a single isolated non-terminal, and \gamma \in (V \cup \Sigma)*.
- Distinction: Unlike Type-1, the derivation of non-terminal A is independent of surrounding symbols (context-free).
- Recognizing Automaton: Non-deterministic Pushdown Automaton (PDA).
- Applications: Compilers, syntax analysis, parsing programming languages (Backus-Naur Form).
- Canonical Example: The palindrome language L = {w w^R | w \in {0, 1}*} and balanced parentheses.

### Type-3: Regular Grammars
- Production Rule Format: Either strictly right-linear (A \to a B or A \to a) or strictly left-linear.
- Recognizing Automaton: Deterministic / Non-Deterministic Finite State Automata (DFA / NFA).
- Expressiveness: Most restricted class; cannot perform unbounded counting.

## Section 4.4: Pumping Lemma for Context-Free Languages
For any context-free language L, there exists a pumping length p \ge 1 such that any string s \in L with |s| \ge p can be divided into five pieces s = uvxyz satisfying:
1. |vxy| \le p
2. |vy| \ge 1 (i.e., at least one of v or y is non-empty)
3. For all i \ge 0, u v^i x y^i z \in L.
This lemma is used to prove that languages such as {a^n b^n c^n} are NOT context-free, and thus require context-sensitive grammars.
